\section*{What was wrong}

A reduced elastic body is a floating frame ($6$ DOF) plus $n_m$ modal
coordinates. Commit \texttt{06275246} merged the two into one $(6+n_m)$ dense
block. Positive definiteness of that block requires its three partitions
$\mathbf{H}_{ff}$, $\mathbf{H}_{fm}$, $\mathbf{H}_{mm}$ to come from a single
$\mathbf{J}^\top\mathbf{K}\mathbf{J}$. They did not.

\textbf{Each elastic contact was evaluated twice, on different geometry.}

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
 & $\mathbf{H}_{ff}$ (frame) & $\mathbf{H}_{fm},\ \mathbf{H}_{mm}$ (cross, modal) \\
\midrule
evaluator & \texttt{...\_from\_collision} & \texttt{...\_from\_world\_points} \\
contact point & local point $\times$ rigid \texttt{body\_q} & deformed, sample $+\sum_i q_i\bm{\phi}_i$ \\
levers & split (skeleton / anchor) & one lever \\
damping gate & rigid approach rate & deformed approach rate \\
friction slip & rigid anchor & deformed sample \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The caller computed the deformed point, used it for the scalar penetration, and
then passed the \emph{local} point to the frame evaluator. Everything entering
$\mathbf{H}_{ff}$ was therefore computed on the undeformed blade.

Fitting a contact stiffness
$\mathbf{K}=a(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{n}\mathbf{n}^\top)+b\,\mathbf{n}\mathbf{n}^\top$
separately from each side over $62$ blocks gives
$a_{\text{elastic}}/a_{\text{frame}}$ between $0.4$ and $14$, median $0.6$ ---
not a constant, so not a units or duplication bug. The resulting deficiency is
two-dimensional in $90\%$ of indefinite blocks and lies in the friction tangent
plane (overlap with the contact normal $0.000$--$0.021$).

Consequences: $\lambda_{\min}$ down to $-6.5\times10^{8}$;
\texttt{wp.tile\_cholesky} returns a finite but meaningless factor; the only
guard is an \texttt{isfinite} check that never fires; the applied step has
$\|\mathbf{H}\bm{\delta}+\mathbf{g}\|/\|\mathbf{g}\|\approx 1$ instead of
$10^{-6}$. Both rigid solve paths were affected.

The sparse path added a second, independent defect. It solved the full
articulation including the blade and then \emph{discarded} the elastic delta
without pinning the block first, so applying only $\bm{\delta}_r$ left
\[
  \mathbf{g}_r^{+} = (1-\omega)\,\mathbf{g}_r - \omega\,\mathbf{H}_{re}\,\bm{\delta}_e,
  \qquad \|\mathbf{H}_{re}\|\sim k = 10^{6},
\]
a spurious force on every neighbour each iteration. That is why the sparse path
reached $10^{15}$ while the local path, running the same broken block, stayed
bounded.

\textbf{Why it surfaced only after the port.} Both evaluators, and the
inconsistency between them, are byte-identical before and after
\texttt{06275246}; the port changed neither. Before the merge the two results
went into \emph{separate} matrices, each individually positive definite, so the
disagreement was a block Gauss--Seidel splitting error that cost convergence
rate and nothing else. The merge put them in one matrix and silently promoted it
into a violated definiteness precondition. This is unique to this branch: the
upstream elastic branches have no AVBD contact --- no dual, no hard contacts, no
stabilization --- so both call sites go through the deformed-point evaluator and
\texttt{...\_from\_collision} is never called at all.

\section*{What the changes do}

\textbf{1. One evaluation, three projections} (\texttt{rigid\_vbd\_kernels.py}).
A new \texttt{evaluate\_contact\_force\_hessian\_blocks} dispatches on whether
the contact is elastic:

\begin{lstlisting}
if is_elastic:
    return evaluate_rigid_contact_from_world_points(...)  # deformed points
return evaluate_rigid_contact_from_collision(...)         # rigid, unchanged
\end{lstlisting}

Both frame call sites (\texttt{accumulate\_body\_body\_contacts\_per\_body},
\texttt{compute\_rigid\_contact\_forces}) now go through it, passing the
deformed points they already computed and previously discarded. An elastic
contact is therefore evaluated by the same function with the same arguments on
the frame side and the modal side, so the block is a partition of one
$\mathbf{J}^\top\mathbf{K}\mathbf{J}$ by construction. Rigid contacts keep
\texttt{from\_collision} untouched, with AVBD's split levers and duals.

\textbf{2. The frame is excluded from the sparse system}
(\texttt{rigid\_sparse\_articulation\_kernels.py}). The mechanism already
existed for kinematic bodies; elastic bodies now use it:

\begin{lstlisting}
if body_inv_mass[body] == 0.0 or body_elastic_index[body] >= 0:
    diag = _mat66_identity(1.0e30)
\end{lstlisting}

The body stays in the sparse structure, so joints reaching it still assemble and
still act on its neighbours, but its own six rows are pinned, the solve returns
$\bm{\delta}_e\approx 0$, and the delta is not applied. The port had the discard
without the pin, which is what made $\mathbf{H}_{re}\bm{\delta}_e$ large; with
the pin the discarded delta is genuinely zero. This mirrors the local path,
where \texttt{solve\_rigid\_body} has always returned early on elastic bodies.

\textbf{3. The elastic solve is unified in both paths}
(\texttt{solver\_vbd.py}). \texttt{solve\_frame=True} at all three sparse launch
sites. Frame and modes are solved together, once, from one contact evaluation,
in both the local and the block-sparse path.

\section*{Verification}

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
\toprule
 & \textbf{before} & \textbf{frame excluded} & \textbf{unified (now)} \\
\midrule
indefinite blocks (of $320$) & $132$ & $0$ (trivially $\mathbf{I}_6$) & $\mathbf{0}$ \\
$\lambda_{\min}$             & $-6.5\times10^{8}$ & $+1.0$ & $+1.88\times10^{5}$ \\
linear residual              & $0.47$--$1.25$ & $10^{-7}$ & $\le 1.2\times10^{-5}$ \\
max Cauchy--Schwarz ratio    & $3.66$ & $0$ & $\mathbf{0.9962}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The sparse and local paths now produce identical block statistics. The
Cauchy--Schwarz ratio sits just inside the bound and never crosses it, which is
what a genuine $\mathbf{J}^\top\mathbf{K}\mathbf{J}$ looks like when a contact
couples the frame and the modes almost maximally. Over $90$ frames both paths
stay bounded and agree (base $z$: sparse $0.640\to0.436$, local
$0.642\to0.187$). \texttt{test\_vbd\_sparse\_articulation} passes $14/14$.

\section*{Still open}

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{No guard in the tiled solve.} The \texttt{isfinite} check should
        be a residual check ($\|\mathbf{H}\bm{\delta}+\mathbf{g}\| >
        \varepsilon\|\mathbf{g}\|$ falling back to Jacobi). It would have caught
        this on the first iteration of the first substep.
  \item \textbf{No regression test.} A drop test cannot reproduce it: the
        deficiency is in the friction tangent plane and needs lateral slip. The
        cheapest real test asserts $\lambda_{\min}>0$ on the assembled block.
  \item \texttt{test\_reduced\_elastic\_body} reported $17$ failures before this
        work; whether they predate it is unconfirmed.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Dead ends}
\small
Recorded so they are not re-run: stiff two-block Gauss--Seidel ($\rho<1$
throughout, and a spectral radius cannot see a $\bm{\delta}$ that does not solve
$\mathbf{H}\bm{\delta}=-\mathbf{g}$); stale force accumulators; the implicit
frame/modal mass coupling; the neighbour solver ($0.9892$ vs.\ $0.9893$); sign
or index errors in the cross blocks (each is a correct derivative \emph{of its
own evaluation}); the minimal 2D model, too small to reproduce anything. Pinning
the sparse block alone was tried early and still diverged: necessary, not
sufficient.
